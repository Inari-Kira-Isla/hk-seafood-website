f:T8b4,Standard HK Cantonese wedding menus run 8–10 courses. Sea urchin and salmon don't appear in every course — knowing exactly where they fit lets you size orders correctly.

**Course 1: Cold Appetiser Platter (前菜拼盤)**
Most common entry point for premium seafood. Standard composition: jellyfish, drunken chicken, marinated cucumber, century egg. Premium upgrades commonly add:
• Salmon sashimi rosettes — 30–50g per table (8–12 guest table = ~3–4g per guest)
• Smoked salmon canapés — 40–60g per table
• Salmon tartare cups — 30–40g per table

Volume math: 30 tables × 40g = 1.2 kg salmon for a single banquet just for this course

**Course 2: Soup (羹/湯)**
Rarely uses sea urchin or salmon. Skip for sourcing purposes.

**Course 3–4: Hot Seafood (海鮮類)**
Typically lobster, abalone, scallop, garoupa. Salmon as a hot course is uncommon in traditional Cantonese banquets but appears in fusion menus.

**Premium Add-On: Uni Feature (海膽特色菜)**
Increasingly common at 5-star properties as a 'special course' add-on charged at HKD $180–380 per guest:
• Uni rice bowl mini (海膽飯) — 8–12g uni per guest = 100–150g per table
• Uni & scallop sashimi pairing — 10–15g uni per guest = 120–180g per table
• Uni custard (uni chawanmushi style) — 6–8g per guest = 80–100g per table

Volume math: 30 tables × 130g uni = 3.9 kg for one banquet's uni course

**Course 5–6: Poultry, Vegetable, Rice/Noodle**
No seafood typically.

**Course 7: Fish Course (魚)**
Steamed fish — usually grouper, leopard coral grouper, or seasonal whole fish. Not a salmon/uni course.

**Course 8: Dessert (甜品)**
No seafood.

10:Ta0d,Wedding banquet sourcing happens on a different timeline than restaurant operations. The booking-to-event window is the planning horizon.

**Standard HK wedding booking timeline:**

• **12–18 months ahead**: Couple secures venue, signs deposit
• **6–9 months ahead**: Menu tasting, course-level decisions confirmed
• **3–4 months ahead**: Guest count locked, table count finalized, premium upsells confirmed
• **8–12 weeks ahead**: F&B procurement should lock supplier allocation
• **2–3 weeks ahead**: Final guest count tweaks (typically ±1–2 tables)
• **Event week**: Delivery scheduling confirmed, contingency plans in place

**The 8–12 week allocation window is non-negotiable for premium seafood.** This is when:
• Hokkaido uni allocations are still flexible at the importer level
• Salmon week-of-event spot pricing risk is low
• Suppliers can confirm specific grades and origins

**The forecasting calculation banquet F&B teams should run weekly:**

```
For each event in the next 12 weeks:
  Tables × per-table seafood spec = base requirement
  + 10–15% buffer for spoilage/yield
  = order quantity

Aggregate by week:
  Week of [date]: total uni + total salmon required
  Compare to supplier confirmed allocation
  Flag gaps to procurement immediately
```

**Auspicious-date Saturday allocation: the special case**

For weekends carrying 5+ events at one property (or 30+ events across HK), normal allocation falls apart. Practical responses:

• **Lock supplier allocation 16–20 weeks ahead** with written commitment letters specifying volume, grade, and price ceiling
• **Diversify across 2 suppliers** for these dates, splitting volume 70/30 to maintain backup capacity
• **Pre-pay or deposit** for confirmed allocation when supplier requests — this is normal practice for top accounts
• **Specify substitute protocols in writing**: if Hokkaido AA Bafun is unavailable on event day, what is the acceptable substitution path?

11:T92e,Banquet kitchens have narrow operational windows. Delivery timing is as important as product quality.

**The Saturday-night banquet timeline (typical):**

• **Friday morning–afternoon**: Mise en place begins for Saturday events
• **Friday evening**: Cold appetisers prepped, sashimi-cut salmon stored
• **Saturday morning**: Final mise en place, last delivery acceptable
• **Saturday 16:00–18:00**: Setup and pre-banquet prep complete
• **Saturday 18:30–22:30**: Banquet service

**The implication for sourcing:** Salmon and uni for Saturday banquets should arrive Friday morning. This gives the kitchen 24–30 hours of controlled cold storage time before service — enough to allow inspection, rejection if needed, and re-supply if anything fails.

**Delivery scheduling rules of thumb:**

• **Saturday banquet**: deliver Friday 09:00–13:00
• **Sunday banquet**: deliver Saturday 09:00–13:00 (or Friday with longer storage)
• **Auspicious-date weekend (multiple events)**: stagger deliveries Thursday + Friday to spread cold-chain load and reduce rejection risk
• **Mid-week banquet (corporate, smaller weddings)**: deliver day-before, before 12:00

**Cold-chain checklist on receiving (banquet-specific):**

• Temperature on arrival: ≤4°C for salmon, ≤2°C for uni
• Visual inspection: salmon flesh colour, fat marbling, no blood pooling; uni colour vibrancy, no off-odour, no liquid in tray
• Documentation: harvest date, supplier batch number, cold-chain log
• Rejection protocol must be defined in writing in advance — banquet kitchens cannot make rejection decisions ad-hoc on event week

**Backup delivery contingency:**

For major events (50+ tables, auspicious-date Saturdays), the procurement team should pre-arrange:

• Secondary supplier confirmed available with same-day or next-morning emergency delivery capability
• On-property cold storage with verified backup capacity (in case primary cooler fails)
• Clear escalation chain: who decides to invoke contingency, and within what time window

**The expensive lesson learned by HK hotels:** A supplier delivering Friday afternoon for Saturday-night banquet leaves no recovery window. If product fails inspection, there is no time to source replacement. 12:Tad9,Banquet F&B operates on visible per-guest pricing. Understanding the cost structure helps you price upsells correctly and protect margin.

**Per-guest cost breakdown for premium seafood courses:**

**Salmon sashimi cold platter add-on (40g per guest)**
• Salmon cost (sashimi-grade Norwegian/Scottish): HKD $5.50–8.00 per guest at 40g
• Plate-up labour: HKD $2.00 per guest
• Garnish + accoutrements: HKD $1.50 per guest
• Total food cost: HKD $9–11.50 per guest
• Typical menu add-on price: HKD $48–88 per guest
• Gross margin: 78–87%

**Uni feature course (130g per table = 12g per guest at 11-guest table)**
• Uni cost (Hokkaido Murasaki A grade): HKD $1.10–1.40 per gram = HKD $13.20–16.80 per guest
• Premium uni (Bafun AA): HKD $2.20–2.90 per gram = HKD $26.40–34.80 per guest
• Plate-up labour, accompaniments, plating: HKD $5–8 per guest
• Total food cost (Murasaki version): HKD $18–25 per guest
• Total food cost (Bafun version): HKD $32–43 per guest
• Typical menu add-on price: HKD $180–380 per guest
• Gross margin: 80–90%

**Where margin leaks in banquet seafood operations:**

• **Over-ordering**: 15% buffer is standard, 25%+ buffer wastes margin on high-cost items. Track yield-after-trim by supplier and use that data to right-size buffers.
• **Wrong-grade procurement**: ordering Bafun AA when the menu specifies Murasaki A (or vice versa) is a 2x cost differential. Confirm grade specification at the F&B order step, not just verbally.
• **Spot-buying for forgotten upsells**: spot uni purchases at 7-day lead time can run 30–60% above confirmed allocation prices. Build the upsell tracker mentioned earlier to prevent this.
• **Substitution downgrades**: when supplier delivers B-grade or off-origin product because allocation wasn't locked, the kitchen still pays committed prices. Written commitment letters at allocation lock prevent this.
• **Cold-chain failure write-offs**: a single cold-chain incident on a Saturday auspicious date can cost HKD $15,000–40,000 in product loss. Backup delivery and on-property cold storage redundancy are insurance.

13:Tb50,Generic supplier criteria apply (grade, cold-chain, documentation). Banquet operations layer on three additional must-haves:

**1. Volume scaling capability for auspicious-date weekends**

A supplier servicing 3 hotels needs to scale 5x on a single Saturday. Most cannot. Ask:
• What is the largest single-day delivery volume you have executed in the last 12 months?
• How many wedding-banquet accounts do you serve, and what is your peak-Saturday combined volume?
• If our property has a 50-table wedding on the same Saturday as 8 other properties' weddings, can you commit volume to all of us?
• Show me the supplier-side allocation calendar for the next 4 auspicious-date Saturdays

**2. Friday-morning delivery commitment**

Late-Friday or Saturday-morning delivery is unacceptable for major banquet events. Ask:
• Can you commit to delivery before 13:00 on the day before any banquet event?
• What is your contingency if your normal Friday-morning truck fails?
• Do you have a Macau-side or HK-side warehouse that can run emergency deliveries?
• Show me your Friday-morning on-time delivery rate for the last 6 months

**3. Written substitution protocol**

When the planned product is unavailable, the substitution path must be defined in writing before the event. Ask:
• If Hokkaido Bafun AA is unavailable, what specific product will you substitute, at what price?
• Who decides on substitution — your team or ours, and within what time window?
• Is the substitution price-protected to the original commitment, or does it float?
• What is your refund policy if you cannot deliver the committed product or an acceptable substitute?

**Red flags specific to banquet seafood sourcing:**

• Supplier won't commit to specific Friday delivery window — vague "day-before delivery" is not enough
• Supplier resists written allocation commitments for auspicious-date Saturdays
• Supplier substitutes grades or origins without prior notice on regular orders (signal of how they will behave on event week)
• Supplier cannot produce documented Friday-morning delivery rate data
• No clear escalation contact for event-week emergencies (a single sales rep is not enough — there must be ops/dispatch contact)

**The relationship pattern that works for HK banquet operations:**

• Quarterly business reviews focused on the next 13 weeks of confirmed events
• Monthly volume forecasting calls with the F&B team
• Pre-locked allocation for all auspicious-date Saturdays 16+ weeks ahead
• Joint contingency planning for the 8–10 highest-stakes events of the year
• Open invoicing reconciliation and quality issue logging

Suppliers willing to operate at this engagement level are the ones worth committing to. • Lock course composition; confirm seafood premium upsells

• Refine volume calculation

• Final volume committed in writing both directions
• Substitution protocol confirmed in writing
• Backup supplier identified for contingency volume

• Confirm any guest-count tweaks (≤±2 tables typical)
• Reconfirm delivery scheduling 72 hours ahead
• Verify on-property cold storage capacity

• Yield analysis: actual usage vs. ordered, by product
• Quality feedback to supplier (positive AND negative)
